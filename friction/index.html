Theory

Why one angle is all you need

As the board tilts, static friction grows to match the pull of gravity down the slope. It can only grow up to μsN. The angle where it runs out gives μs directly (Giancoli, §4–8).

Along the boardmg sin θ − fs = 0
PerpendicularN − mg cos θ = 0
Just before slipfs = μsN  at  θ = θc
Combinemg sin θc = μs mg cos θc
Resultμs = tan θc

The mass cancels. Move the mass slider on the right: both curves stretch, but the point where they cross stays put.

Uncertainty

What does one degree cost?

An angle reading of θ ± Δθ puts μs between tan(θ − Δθ) and tan(θ + Δθ). The same Δθ costs more on a grippy surface, because tan θ gets steeper as θ grows.

The project's own example, 30° ± 1°, gives a coefficient between tan 29° and tan 31°. Drag the reading to 45° with the same ±1° and the band gets noticeably taller.

Δμs ≈ sec²θ · Δθ

Here Δθ is in radians. At 30°, sec²θ is about 1.33; at 45° it is 2. This is why the analysis compares the spread of repeated trials with the spread that the angle device alone would cause: if two surfaces or two masses differ by less than both, the difference means nothing.

Method

Procedure

Board about 0.5–1.0 m long, a short wide block with extra weights near its centre, two surface materials, a balance, and a protractor or calibrated inclinometer.

  1. Prepare surface A

    Record the material, check it lies flat on the board, and mark the starting position.

  2. Weigh the masses

    Weigh the block alone and with its added loads. Record M1, M2 and M3 and keep the same totals on both surfaces.

  3. Zero the angle device

    Zero it on a horizontal reference. A phone used as an inclinometer stays clear of the sliding path.

  4. Raise the board slowly

    Start M1 at the mark. Lift without pushing the block, and use only small angle changes near the sliding point.

  5. Read θc

    Record the angle at the first continuous slide. A brief jump does not count.

  6. Repeat five times

    Reset and repeat for five readings, then do M2 and M3 with the same contact face and orientation.

  7. Switch to surface B

    Record the new material and repeat all fifteen measurements with the same block, masses and method.

  8. Note anything odd

    Toppling, a moving surface sheet, or any unexpected result goes in the observations column.
